WHIT 202606
6 rules

get the look

The rules behind every Fetch email after the makeover, shown on the Fetch Amex launch.

1Keep the visuals comingImagery carries the story all the way down, not just in the hero.
2Tell a storyEmails that read like content, with a narrative from header to footer instead of a flyer and a wall of text.
3Design for thumbsF-pattern friendly structure and clear hierarchy for people reading on their phones.
4Give them ways to clickEvery module links somewhere, so one email can drive to several offers and features.
5Go modularA library of interchangeable modules, so every send is assembled, not designed from scratch.
6Mix and matchSwappable versions of each module, so people who get the same flow repeatedly don't see the same email twice.

trendreport

The best briefs come from culture. I track what’s on the rise, from TikTok beauty looks to the zodiac’s Gen Z comeback, and find the angle that lets Fetch add to the moment while it’s still building.

TikTok

Trend 01 · TikTok, blog, email

Latte makeup + dupe culture

Two beauty trends were peaking at once: the bronzy latte makeup look on TikTok, and dupes of viral high-end products everywhere. I put them together in one campaign: how to get the latte look with dupes. Every dupe came from our L'Oréal partner brands, so shoppers earned Fetch points on the exact products in the tutorial.

“Two rising trends, plus an angle only Fetch could bring: points on every dupe. That’s how a brand adds to culture.”

WHIT 202611

somethingborrowed

The challenge

Brideside was known for bridesmaid dresses. Brideside BRIDE and its showrooms asked brides to trust that same brand with the one dress that matters most.

The insight

A wedding dress is a bigger purchase with a pickier buyer. Trust had to come first.

The strategy

A new bridal brand can’t build credibility overnight, so we borrowed it. The launch leaned on partners brides already followed and trusted, from the internet’s favorite wedding commentators to a celebrity stylist.

“In bridal, trust comes from voices brides already follow. So we borrowed some.”

WHIT · 202622

Ask Whit

When did you know strategy was your thing?

My first year at Fetch. I was hired as a designer, but I kept asking why. I’d hear the thinking behind a campaign, then look at the final work and know a shopper would never pick up on it. So I started pushing. When a stack of brands each wanted their own Father’s Day email, I pitched one Fetch Father’s Day package where every brand got its moment. When Monster, Red Bull and another energy drink all came in at once, I turned three literal asks into one story about boosting your morning, and brought the blog and social teams in to tell it.

A brief lands on your desk. What happens next?

First I write it as one sentence: this brand wants to achieve ___ through Fetch. Then I lock the goal, the KPI and the guardrails. Then I beat up the business problem until I find the human one underneath it. What does the shopper need that also gets the brand what it wants? That’s where the research starts, and where the story shows up.

What’s your unpopular opinion?

If you’re talking to everyone, you’re talking to no one. I believe in specificity: know exactly who you’re speaking to, stand on a clear identity and let your community find you. Nobody wants to be advertised to, so stop squeezing into their spaces and give them a reason to come to you.

How do you know an idea is the one?

I picture saying it out loud to a room. If people go “oh” or “yes,” it’s hitting a real lived experience. Then I ask what it does beyond the obvious. A moisturizer moisturizes. But does it look pretty on the shelf? Do you feel put-together pulling it out of your bag? How someone feels using it is the real product.

What’s the dream project?

A brand that makes girls like me feel seen. I think about middle-school me in her room, flipping through Seventeen and dreaming up pages of her own. I want to build something that gives that girl a community.

Which brands have you worked with?

Fetch, Chewy, e.l.f., Hershey, Albertsons, DoorDash, General Mills, Amazon, Dr Pepper, Cheez-It, Pepsi, Kraft Heinz, Brideside, Dana Rebecca Designs, Modern Luxury, Social Sparkling Wine and more.

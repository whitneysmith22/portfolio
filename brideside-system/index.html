Brideside:
Brand to System

I inherited a logo, a couple of typefaces and one blue. I helped shape the brand, then built the system that made every email and ad look like the same brand.

My roleCreative lead across three sub-brands
ChannelsEmail, Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest
Year2019–2020

The challenge

Brideside had loose brand guidelines: a logo, typefaces and one brand blue. With three expressions of the brand (Brideside, the Poppy sub-brand and the Brideside BRIDE bridal line), weekly sends and always-on paid social, there was nothing to keep the creative consistent or fast to produce.

The brand book

Branding ’19, the identity I helped develop with Brideside leadership and our agency partner when I joined. One brand, two worlds, each written like a moment from the wedding day.

The signature devices

Then I turned the brand into a marketing system: a small kit of repeatable moves. Any designer could combine them and still land on-brand.

Echo typeBold lowercase headlines with offset pastel shadows. Instantly recognizable, even as a thumbnail.
Highlighter barsThe iridescent gradient, swiped under headings and buttons.
Sideways labelsRotated section labels that turn any module into a chapter.
Offset color blocks + name tagsPhotos layered on seasonal color blocks, with the product name tagged right on the image.
Taped, tilted photosA scrapbook touch for softer, editorial moments.
The Total Package footerA dark closing module that carried the offer in every bridal send.

Beyond email

The system carried onto Facebook, Instagram and Pinterest. One "See yourself at" template served both the bridesmaid and bridal businesses with a simple color swap.

Creative as research

Paid social was as much a research tool as a performance channel. Because the system held everything else constant, each test isolated one variable, from the offer and the hook to the color story and the gown in the photo.

The insights shaped more than the ads. They informed how we understood the Brideside customer and what she responded to, emotionally and aesthetically.

What we learned

Brides and bridesmaids buy for different reasons. The value props that moved one audience didn't always move the other.

Taste has a map and an age. Affinities for certain styles and designers shifted by region and age, so we could match the right products to the right audiences.
